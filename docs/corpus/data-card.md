# Data Card: HeavDen synthetic inpatient data

> **All data is synthetic.** No real patient was involved. Names, dates of birth and identifiers are generated by Synthea and are fictional.

## 1. Summary

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Patients | ~2,000 synthetic adults from **Synthea** (Massachusetts population model, seed 42), of whom about 300 are in hospital at any time |
| Hospitals | 3 sites, 360 monitored beds (see the *Site and Unit Handbook*) |
| Vital signs | 6 vitals every 5 minutes from wearable monitors, about 86,000 messages per day |
| Nurse observations | ACVPU consciousness and oxygen, every 1-4 hours depending on NEWS2 |
| Outcomes | escalation events (rapid response call or ICU transfer), known 6 hours after each prediction |
| Training table | one row per patient per hour (`gold.patient_hour_features`), ~1% positive |

## 2. How the data is made

1. **Synthea** generates people with realistic histories: conditions (COPD, heart failure, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, hypertension, atrial fibrillation), medications and encounters.
2. **Admissions:** older and sicker people are more likely to be admitted, and are placed on a site, ward and bed. Patients are admitted, transferred and discharged over time (median stay about 4.5 days), and wearable devices are reused between patients.
3. **Vital-signs simulator:** each patient has a personal baseline shaped by their conditions (e.g. COPD lowers SpO2, beta-blockers lower heart rate). Vitals wander slowly, follow a day/night rhythm, and fever raises heart and breathing rates. The *device* then adds noise, motion artefacts, stuck values, dropped messages and battery outages.
4. **Truth generator:** a **hidden rule** decides which patients start to deteriorate (respiratory, sepsis or cardiac). Their vitals worsen over 2-12 hours, and staff escalate when the deterioration reaches the protocol's threshold. The model never sees the hidden rule, only the vitals and outcomes.
5. **Bedside care:** nurses give oxygen to patients with low SpO2 (which hides the drop), and record consciousness.

## 3. Labels

A patient-hour at time `t` is **positive** if an escalation happens in the following 6 hours, (t, t+6h]. The label stays unknown (null) until the window has closed. Escalations occur at about 3-4 per 100 patient-days.

## 4. Splits

Data is split **by time**: days 1-9 for training, 10-11 for validation (model choice and calibration), 12-14 for the final test, with 6-hour gaps between blocks so look-ahead windows don't straddle them. Random splits are not used because neighbouring hours of the same patient would leak between blocks.

## 5. Drift scenarios

The generator can switch on four controlled changes, used to test monitoring (see the *Drift Incident Runbook*):
- SpO2 sensor bias at Site B (data drift)
- respiratory outbreak (population drift)
- escalation protocol change (concept drift)
- firmware schema change (schema drift)

## 6. Known simplifications

- Deteriorations follow smooth curves; real ones are messier.
- Oxygen treats the symptom only: it doesn't change whether a patient is escalated.
- Only site, age, sex and ward type are available for fairness analysis. Ethnicity and other attributes are not simulated.
- No children, maternity, ICU or emergency department patients.
- One Synthea run; rare conditions have few examples.

## 7. Governance

Data is labelled synthetic everywhere it appears. Even so, Unity Catalog applies the same controls as for real data: row filters by site, masks on name and date of birth, and least-privilege service principals. This shows the governance pattern without any real personal data.
